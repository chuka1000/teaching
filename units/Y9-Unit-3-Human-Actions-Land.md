# Y9 Unit 3 — Human Actions and the Land

**Class:** `9G` or `9I` — set the code before building. **3 hours** · three
50-minute singles (150 min), leaving about 30 minutes for assessment.

Two objectives across three hours is comfortable, so the time goes on making
the second one concrete rather than on covering more ground.

| # | Lesson | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What the land gives us | Identify land resources |
| 2 | Losing the soil | How human actions affect land |
| 3 | Cities, and what can be done | How human actions affect land |

---

## Read this before building

**This unit follows straight on from Human Population Growth**, and that lesson
already covered some of the ground: its impacts slide said land is cleared for
food and habitats are lost. Open
`reference/Human Population Growth.pptx` and check what was said before writing
Lesson 1, or the Do Now and the I Do will repeat it.

The difference in emphasis: Unit 2 asked *what happens when there are more
people*. This unit asks *what happens to the land itself* — the soil, not the
species.

**Spelling.** The unit plan writes "urbanization". Both endings are valid
British English, but pick one and hold it across slides, worksheet and board.
Recommend **urbanisation**, matching the -ise convention students meet in the
rest of their school writing. Whichever is chosen, never mix the two.

**Do not make this a doom unit.** Lesson 3 should end on what works, not on a
list of damage. Year 9 disengage fast from a lesson that says everything is
ruined and nothing can be done.

**Verify the figures before they go on a slide.** Two in particular are worth
checking rather than recalling: the rate at which soil forms, and the
proportion of the world's population now living in cities. Both are commonly
quoted wrongly.

Keep examples global. The American Dust Bowl, the Sahel, the Aral Sea and any
fast-growing city outside Thailand all work.

## Lesson 1

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      What the land gives us

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Identify the main land resources.
  2. Explain what soil is and why it matters.
  3. Explain why soil counts as a non-renewable resource on a human timescale.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Human Population Growth.pptx — read it first. It already
            covered land being cleared for food. Build on that; do not repeat it.
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Listing resources as a memorisation exercise. Objective 3 is the
            one that earns the lesson.
```

Vocabulary: land resource.

Objective 3 is the hook and the point: soil looks like an endless supply of
dirt, and it forms so slowly that on any timescale a person cares about it does
not come back. That reframes everything in Lessons 2 and 3.

## Lesson 2

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Losing the soil

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Describe what erosion is and what causes it.
  2. Describe what desertification is.
  3. Explain how farming can cause both.

PREVIOUS:   reference/What The Land Gives Us.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  Erosion trays. Two trays of soil at the same slope, one bare and
            one with grass or leaf cover. Pour the same amount of water down
            each and compare what runs off.
            Equipment: two shallow trays, soil, turf or leaves, two jugs, two
            catch pots. Ten minutes including the comparison.
```

Vocabulary: erosion, desertification.

The misconception to target: that these are the same thing. Erosion is soil
being removed. Desertification is dry land degrading until it stops supporting
what it used to — erosion is one cause of it, not a synonym.

The second: desertification does not mean existing deserts spreading outwards.
It means land becoming desert-like, usually where people are using it.

## Lesson 3

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Cities, and what can be done

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Describe what urbanisation is.
  2. Explain how building on land changes it.
  3. Describe two things that reduce damage to land.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Losing The Soil.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Ending on damage. Objective 3 is the closing note and needs real
            examples, not "we should recycle".
```

Vocabulary: urbanisation.

Objective 1 has a definition worth being precise about: urbanisation is the
rising *proportion* of people living in cities, not simply cities getting
bigger.

Objective 2 is the concrete one — covering soil with a hard surface stops water
soaking in, which moves the problem downstream as flooding rather than removing
it.

Objective 3 examples that hold up: terracing, contour ploughing, cover crops,
tree planting to hold soil, building upwards rather than outwards. Each should
be tied back to a specific problem from Lesson 2 so it reads as a solution to
something rather than a list.

This is the last lesson of the unit, so the plenary closes it rather than
pointing forward.
