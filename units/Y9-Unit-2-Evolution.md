## Read this before building any of them

**Handle the topic straight.** Some students will come from families where
evolution is contested. Teach it as the scientific consensus supported by the
evidence, which is what it is. Do not stage a debate about belief, do not
single anyone out, and do not add a "some people disagree" slide. The science
stands on its own and the lesson is about the evidence.

**The misconception that eats this whole unit is teleology** — the idea that
organisms change because they need to, want to, or are trying to. Every lesson
should push against it. Giraffes did not stretch their necks. Bacteria do not
decide to resist antibiotics. Variation comes first; the environment then does
the selecting.

Others worth targeting:

- Evolution happens to **populations**, not to individuals.
- "Fittest" means best suited to the environment, not strongest or fastest.
- Humans did not evolve from monkeys — we share a common ancestor.
- "Only a theory" — a scientific theory is the most heavily evidenced kind of
  idea there is.
- Genetic drift is **random**. Natural selection is not.

**Verify the Darwin history before it goes on a slide.** The popular story —
that the Galápagos finches gave Darwin the idea while he was there — is a later
simplification. He did not label the specimens by island, and the identification
happened after he got home. Check it rather than repeat the version in most
textbooks.

Keep examples global. Peppered moths, antibiotic resistance, Galápagos,
Darwin's voyage.

## Lesson 1

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Natural selection: Darwin's big idea

OBJECTIVES:
  1. State Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection.
  2. Explain the four things natural selection needs: variation,
     competition, survival, inheritance.
  3. Explain why evolution happens to populations, not individuals.

PREVIOUS:   <the last Ecosystems lesson, or "none — first of the unit">
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Any wording that suggests an organism changes on purpose or in
            response to need. No "adapts to" meaning "changes itself".
```

Vocabulary: evolution, natural selection.

The four-step structure is the spine of the whole unit. Get it clean here and
Lessons 3, 6, 7 and 8 all become applications of it.

## Lesson 2

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      How Darwin got there

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Identify the influences on Darwin's thinking.
  2. Explain how artificial selection gave Darwin a model for
     natural selection.
  3. Describe the part Alfred Russel Wallace played.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Natural Selection.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      The tidy legend of the finches. See the note above.
```

The Beagle, Lyell's geology, Malthus on population, and pigeon breeding.

Wallace is the lesson's second idea and worth real time: he reached the same
conclusion independently, and the 1858 joint presentation is why both names
belong on it. Verify the dates before they go on a slide.

## Lesson 3

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Natural selection in action

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Explain a real example of natural selection using the four steps.
  2. Explain why antibiotic resistance is natural selection happening now.
  3. Write an explanation that avoids saying an organism changed on purpose.

PREVIOUS:   reference/How Darwin Got There.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  The beak game. Tweezers, spoons and forks as "beaks", dried beans
            as food, 30 seconds per round. Whichever beak collects least is
            removed. Run three rounds and record the numbers.
            Equipment: tweezers, spoons, forks, dried beans, small pots.
```

Peppered moths and antibiotic resistance.

Objective 3 is the assessed one and the hardest. A correct answer never says
the bacteria *became* resistant — some already were, and the antibiotic killed
the rest.

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      More evidence: bones, embryos and DNA

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Explain what homologous structures show.
  2. Describe what similarities in early development suggest.
  3. Explain why DNA is the strongest evidence we have.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Fossils And The Fossil Record.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>
```

The pentadactyl limb across a human, a whale, a bat and a horse is the single
best image in this unit. Same bones, different jobs.

The argument to land: four independent lines of evidence — fossils, anatomy,
development, DNA — all point the same way. That agreement is the point.

Vocabulary: gene pool, genetic drift.

most in small populations — a coin-flip demonstration with 6 versus 600 makes
it visible.

## Lesson 9

```
CLASS:      <9G | 9I>
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Geologic time

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Describe what the geologic time scale is.
  2. Place a few major events on it.
  3. Explain why the scale is divided the way it is.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Speciation.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  A scale timeline — a length of till roll or a corridor, marked to
            scale. Students place events on it. The point is how late almost
            everything is.
```

Vocabulary: geologic time scale.

4.6 billion years is not a number anyone can feel, which is why it has to be
walked or unrolled rather than read.

Objective 3: the divisions are not arbitrary — the boundaries are mass
extinctions and other large changes in the fossil record. That ties the whole
unit together, so the plenary should close the unit rather than point forward.
