## Lesson 1

```
CLASS:      10A
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Mass and weight

OBJECTIVES:
  1. State that mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object. (P1.3.1)
  2. State that weight is the gravitational force on an object that has mass. (P1.3.2)
  3. Define g as gravitational force per unit mass, and use g = W/m,
     with g ≈ 9.8 N/kg near the Earth's surface. (P1.3.3)

PREVIOUS:   reference/Equations of Motion.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  Teacher demo during the I Do: hang 100 g, 200 g, 500 g from a
            newtonmeter. Readings of about 1, 2 and 5 N. The worksheet gives a
            data table of mass against weight to plot — the gradient is g.

AVOID:      g = 10 N/kg. Any wording that uses "weight" to mean mass.
```

Hook: *"I weigh 60 kg."* What's wrong with it? Kilograms are mass. Their weight
is 60 × 9.8 = **588 N**.

The misconception to kill: mass and weight are the same thing measured
differently. The We Do should have at least one row on units — kg for mass,
N for weight.

Worth a line in the notes: the gradient of the W against m graph is g, which
is the gradient skill from *Motion Graphs* in a new place.

## Lesson 2

```
CLASS:      10A
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Gravitational fields and free fall

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Describe weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass. (P1.3.4)
  2. Know that gravitational field strength is equivalent to the
     acceleration of free fall. (P1.3.5)
  3. Show that N/kg and m/s² are the same unit.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Mass and Weight.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Air resistance and terminal velocity — not this section. State
            "ignore air resistance" wherever free fall is used.
```

This is **Supplement** content. If every student in 10A is sitting Core,
drop this lesson and fold the Moon example into Lesson 1's Gold tier instead.

The payoff: the acceleration they calculated in *Equations of Motion* and read
off velocity–time graphs **is** g. A dropped object's velocity–time graph is a
straight line with gradient 9.8 m/s².

Units: 1 N = 1 kg m/s², so 1 N/kg = 1 m/s². Same number, same quantity.

Moon example: g ≈ 1.6 N/kg. The 60 kg student still has a mass of 60 kg there,
but weighs 60 × 1.6 = **96 N**. Mass does not change; weight does.
