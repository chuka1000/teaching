# Y7 Unit 2 — Scientific Research and Technology

**Class:** `7B` · **6 hours** · seven 50-minute singles (350 min).
7B has no doubles in either week, so every lesson is a standard single.

Twelve objectives in six hours is tight. The grouping below pairs objectives
that genuinely belong together rather than spreading them evenly.

| # | Lesson | Objectives covered |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How scientists investigate | steps of an investigation; staying safe (recap) |
| 2 | Fair tests | variables, control, replication |
| 3 | Measuring properly | how measurements are made; accuracy; Kelvin |
| 4 | Records and honesty | keeping good records; ethics |
| 5 | Big numbers and small ones | significant figures; scientific notation |
| 6 | Averages, graphs and models | descriptive statistics; data graphs; role of models |
| 7 | Technology and society | define technology; design process; science ↔ technology ↔ society |

## Two things worth knowing before you start

**The measuring practical belongs in Lesson 3.** The scheme of work lists "lab
equipment orientation and measurement practice" as a Unit 1 activity, but no
Unit 1 objective mentions equipment or measurement — Unit 2 objective 3 does.
Lesson 3 is where it lands.

**Safety is a recap, not new teaching.** They did Bunsen burners and the lab
safety brief in Unit 1, Lesson 4 (*Into The Lab*). Objective 8 is a five-minute
retrieval at the top of Lesson 1, not a lesson of its own.

## Lesson 1

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      How scientists investigate

OBJECTIVES:
  1. List the steps of a scientific investigation.
  2. Write a hypothesis that can actually be tested.
  3. Recall the safety rules for working in the lab.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Into The Lab.pptx — they have used Bunsen burners and
            know the safety routine. Objective 3 is retrieval, not new
            teaching; keep it to the Do Now.
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Do not present the steps as a rigid sequence that every scientist
            follows in order. Unit 1 Lesson 1 taught that science is evidence
            and logic, not a recipe. The steps are a useful shape, not a law.
```

Vocabulary: hypothesis, experiment, observation, field study.

The distinction worth the time: an **experiment** controls the variables, a
**field study** cannot. Both are real science.

## Lesson 2

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Fair tests: the three kinds of variable

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Identify the manipulated variable in an investigation.
  2. Identify the responding variable.
  3. Explain why controls and replication matter.

PREVIOUS:   reference/How Scientists Investigate.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  Short design task, not a full practical — give them a question
            ("does a paper aeroplane fly further if it is heavier?") and have
            them name the three kinds of variable before anyone builds
            anything.
```

Vocabulary: manipulated variable, responding variable, control, replication.

This is the most useful lesson in the unit and the one that transfers to every
practical they will ever do. Worth the We Do and Cold Call both being on
spotting variables in described investigations.

## Lesson 3

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Measuring properly

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Choose the right instrument for a measurement.
  2. Explain what accuracy means.
  3. Convert between the Celsius and Kelvin scales.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Fair Tests.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  The measurement circuit — rulers, measuring cylinders,
            thermometers, balances. Stations around the room, each with a
            thing to measure and a results table to fill in.
            Equipment: as above, plus a stopwatch station.
```

Vocabulary: accuracy, Kelvin scale.

Kelvin: freezing 273 K, boiling 373 K, so K = °C + 273. Keep it to that.

The meniscus on a measuring cylinder and reading a ruler from zero rather than
from the end are the two things that actually go wrong.

## Lesson 4

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Records and honesty

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Record results in a clear, complete table.
  2. Explain why a scientist records what actually happened.
  3. Describe what ethics means in scientific research.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Measuring Properly.pptx — they have a set of their own
            measurements from the practical. Use their real data.
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Do not turn ethics into a lecture on cheating. At this age the
            useful version is: you write down what happened, including the
            reading that looks wrong, because the reading that looks wrong is
            sometimes the discovery.
```

Vocabulary: ethics, replication (revisited).

Callback worth making: Unit 1, Lesson 3 was about names left off the record.
This lesson is about the record itself. Same idea, different end of it.

## Lesson 5

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Big numbers and small ones

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Write a number to a given number of significant figures.
  2. Write a large or small number in scientific notation.
  3. Choose a sensible number of figures for a measurement.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Records And Honesty.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

AVOID:      Do not use significant-figure examples with trailing zeros before
            the decimal point (e.g. 4500 to 2 s.f.). Genuinely ambiguous, and
            it is not worth the confusion at Year 7.
```

This is the most maths-like lesson in the unit. Every number in the deck and
the worksheet must be checked with sympy, and the two must agree.

Objective 3 is the one that matters: a ruler reading 12.4 cm should not become
12.400 cm because a calculator said so.

## Lesson 6

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Averages, graphs and models

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Calculate the mean and the range of a set of results.
  2. Choose the right graph for a set of data, and plot it.
  3. Explain what a model is for in science.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Big Numbers And Small Ones.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  Plotting, using their own measurement data from Lesson 3 if it is
            still usable. Squared paper or pre-printed axes.
```

Vocabulary: mean.

Bar chart for categories, line graph for a continuous variable. That one rule
covers almost everything they will meet before Year 10.

The models objective lands here because a graph *is* a model — a simplified
thing that stands in for the real one. That connection is worth making
explicitly rather than teaching models as a separate topic.

## Lesson 7

```
CLASS:      7B
SLOT:       single
TOPIC:      Technology and society

OBJECTIVES:
  1. Define technology, and say how it differs from science.
  2. Outline the technological design process.
  3. Describe how technology and society change each other.

PREVIOUS:   reference/Averages Graphs And Models.pptx
THEY FOUND HARD: <fill in>

PRACTICAL:  Short design task — state a problem, sketch a solution, say how it
            would be tested. Twelve minutes, not a full build.

AVOID:      Keep the examples global rather than Thailand-specific.
```

Vocabulary: technology, technological design, engineer.

The distinction to land: **science asks what is true, technology asks what
works.** A scientist wants to know why something happens; an engineer wants it
to happen on demand.

This is the last lesson of the unit, so the plenary closes Unit 2 rather than
pointing forward.
